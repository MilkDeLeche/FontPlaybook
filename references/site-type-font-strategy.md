# Site-Type Font Strategy

Use this reference when a site's domain or tone should drive font choice.

## Trust-Heavy Sites

Examples: banks, fintech, insurance, healthcare, legal, government, security, B2B procurement.

Goal: make the user feel the organization is competent, stable, careful, and easy to understand.

Best directions:

- Neutral or humanist sans-serif for body and UI.
- Clear numerals and tabular number support for balances, rates, statements, and tables.
- Conservative weight use: regular body, medium labels, semibold headings.
- Strong readability over novelty.

Good candidates:

- Public Sans for sober public-service trust.
- Inter for modern product trust.
- Source Sans 3 for readable institutional content.
- IBM Plex Sans for technical/institutional confidence.
- DM Sans for softer consumer fintech.
- A premium grotesk-inspired alternative when licensed brand polish is required.

Avoid:

- Playful geometric fonts for body text.
- Fragile thin weights.
- Extreme display faces.
- High-contrast fashion serifs unless used very sparingly in a brand campaign.

## SaaS and Product UI

Examples: dashboards, admin tools, CRM, productivity apps, analytics, AI tools, developer platforms.

Goal: make information scannable, calm, modern, and efficient.

Best directions:

- One strong UI sans across the interface.
- Optional mono for code, IDs, metrics, keyboard shortcuts, logs, or developer content.
- High x-height and clear numerals.
- Tight but readable type scale.

Good candidates:

- Inter for a reliable default.
- Geist Sans for crisp developer/product feel.
- Mona Sans for flexible product polish.
- Manrope for data-heavy modern UI.
- Figtree for warmer SaaS.
- Public Sans for utilitarian dashboards.

Avoid:

- Decorative heading faces inside dense tools.
- Too many font sizes or weights.
- Letter-spaced uppercase labels everywhere.

## Hero and Marketing Landing Pages

Examples: startup homepage, launch page, product campaign, creator landing page, waitlist page.

Goal: establish personality quickly while preserving readable body copy and clear CTAs.

Best directions:

- Use a strong display treatment for H1/H2.
- Keep body, buttons, nav, forms, and pricing readable.
- Pair a distinctive display sans with a neutral body sans only if the page needs personality.
- Use fewer weights and bigger contrast in size/spacing.

Good candidates:

- Space Grotesk for distinctive modern headlines.
- Hubot Sans for technical character.
- Mona Sans for flexible high-impact headings and body.
- Outfit for geometric brand-forward pages.
- General Sans for compact, stylish landing pages.
- Figtree or DM Sans when the brand should feel friendly.

Avoid:

- Making every text element loud.
- Using a display font for long paragraphs.
- Over-tightening mobile H1s until words collide or wrap poorly.

## Editorial, Magazine, and Content Sites

Examples: articles, blogs, newsletters, essays, publishing, thought leadership.

Goal: long-form reading comfort and a memorable editorial voice.

Best directions:

- Consider a serif for article body only when it renders beautifully on screen.
- Use a readable sans for navigation, metadata, UI controls, and labels.
- Keep paragraph measure around `60ch` to `75ch`.
- Use generous line-height and thoughtful heading rhythm.

Good candidates:

- Source Sans 3, Fira Sans, or Work Sans for sans-led editorial.
- A quality serif paired with Inter/Public Sans for stronger editorial identity.
- Space Grotesk or General Sans for publication headings with neutral body.

Avoid:

- Low line-height on paragraphs.
- Tiny captions and metadata.
- Overly delicate serifs for mobile reading.

## Luxury, Fashion, Beauty, and Premium Commerce

Examples: fashion stores, jewelry, fragrance, premium hospitality, galleries.

Goal: create refinement and desire without damaging usability.

Best directions:

- Use display type for hero, product titles, campaign copy, and editorial moments.
- Keep commerce UI, prices, filters, forms, and checkout in a readable sans.
- Allow more whitespace and fewer weights.

Good candidates:

- Premium licensed typefaces when the brand depends on distinctiveness.
- Clean grotesques for quiet luxury.
- Elegant serif display faces for campaign headings, paired with a neutral sans.
- Neue Haas Grotesk-style or Sohne-style inspiration when licensing allows.

Avoid:

- Decorative display fonts in checkout.
- Ultra-light text over images.
- Overusing letter-spacing as a substitute for good type.

## Restaurants, Hospitality, and Local Brands

Examples: restaurant sites, cafes, hotels, local venues, salons, events.

Goal: signal atmosphere quickly and make practical information easy to find.

Best directions:

- Use expressive display type for the brand, hero, menu headings, or event titles.
- Use readable sans for hours, location, booking, menu descriptions, and mobile navigation.
- Choose fonts that match the venue: refined, warm, rustic, playful, or minimal.

Good candidates:

- Figtree or DM Sans for friendly modern local brands.
- Space Grotesk or Outfit for contemporary venues.
- A tasteful serif display paired with a neutral sans for upscale restaurants or hotels.

Avoid:

- Script fonts for body text.
- Hard-to-read menu text on mobile.
- Too many decorative font styles.

## Portfolio, Agency, and Creative Sites

Examples: designer portfolio, studio, photographer, artist, creative agency.

Goal: express taste and point of view while keeping work easy to browse.

Best directions:

- Stronger display choices are acceptable.
- Body and case study text still need reading comfort.
- Use typography to support the work, not compete with it.

Good candidates:

- General Sans, Space Grotesk, Mona Sans, Hubot Sans, Outfit.
- A premium or unusual display face if it reinforces the creator's identity.
- Neutral sans body with expressive headings.

Avoid:

- Making project descriptions hard to read.
- Over-pairing multiple expressive faces.
- Letting typography overpower imagery.

## Playful, Game, Kids, or Entertainment Sites

Examples: games, toys, events, entertainment launches, playful apps.

Goal: create energy and recognition while preserving interface clarity.

Best directions:

- Use playful display faces for titles, scores, badges, and moments of delight.
- Use a readable sans for instructions, menus, settings, and controls.
- Test small sizes carefully.

Good candidates:

- Outfit, Figtree, Lexend, or a purpose-chosen display face plus readable UI sans.
- Use custom drawn lettering or SVG for logos/titles when needed instead of forcing a font everywhere.

Avoid:

- Novelty fonts in controls.
- All-caps playful text for paragraphs.
- Tiny outlined or shadowed text.

## Research Prompts

When internet or browser research is available, use queries like:

- `site type typography examples bank website fonts`
- `Fonts In Use financial website sans serif`
- `Typewolf SaaS font examples`
- `Google Fonts <font name> specimen`
- `<font name> websites using`
- `<brand name> font family`

Research should answer:

- Who uses similar fonts or a similar typographic tone?
- Is the font proven in comparable interfaces?
- Is it free, open-source, hosted, or license-dependent?
- Does it have enough weights, italics, language support, and variable font support?
- What implementation approach is appropriate for this project?
